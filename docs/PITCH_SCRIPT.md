# MVA Safe Rescue: three-minute pitch script

Narration for the six-slide pitch, with the timing for each slide.

## Slide 1: MVA Safe Rescue (0:00-0:25)

A family's data deserves hypotheses that fail safely. In MVA, helping stressed
cells survive can preserve the abnormal clones that create risk. So we ask a
different question from ordinary drug ranking: can a candidate restore
meaningful neural output without increasing chromosome error, clone
persistence, malignant fitness, invasion or chemotherapy resistance?

## Slide 2: A rescue can protect the wrong cells (0:25-0:52)

The published fly experiment shows the failure we want to avoid. Rapamycin
increased the number of aneuploid neural stem cells, and it did not restore
differentiated progeny or brain size. The cells survived without producing
functional output. We use sirolimus as a worked no-go control, and we design
every later decision so that a biochemical improvement can never average away
the risk from abnormal clones.

## Slide 3: The transcriptome did not choose a drug (0:52-1:22)

We then used public transcriptomics as a stress test of our own framework. Six
libraries formed three matched pairs. The locked gene analysis called 179
genes, but zero of twelve evaluable primary modules passed q point one zero.
Several pathway directions changed when a block was left out, and only 58, 72
and 39 gene calls kept both significance and effect thresholds across the three
omissions. We left the module verdict at downgrade and did not turn isolated
HSP, redox or mitophagy hints into a drug ranking.

## Slide 4: Two hypotheses and three controls (1:22-1:52)

That leaves two vehicle-controlled hypotheses. Arimoclomol tests proteostasis
and elamipretide tests mitochondrial function, and the negative evidence on
each stays in the dossier. Sirolimus, everolimus and N-acetylcysteine are
controls that cannot advance: they expose uncoupled rescue, mTOR-class
behavior, and redox or oncology hazards. Neither candidate has to beat the
other. Each has to beat vehicle on every required benefit endpoint without
triggering a safety veto.

## Slide 5: The next experiment can reject a bad idea early (1:52-2:32)

The design stops if authorized local analysis does not support a compatible
partial SAC-deficiency mechanism. If it does, two independent guides must
reproduce the deficient state before candidate testing. Arimoclomol and
elamipretide are then compared with vehicle at two locked doses. Both doses must
improve day-21 live MAP2- and TUBB3-positive neuron yield, ATP-linked oxygen
consumption, and TMRM signal per mitochondrial mass. Any apparent pass requires
second-guide or orthogonal confirmation, and every chromosome,
clone-persistence, invasion, tumor-fitness and chemotherapy-resistance gate
remains a veto. The planning probabilities shown are 37.4 percent for the
0.70-SD, 32-run scenario and 83.8 percent for the 1.00-SD, 24-run scenario.
These numbers cover benefit only and are not sample-size recommendations.
Safety precision sets the final run count.

## Slide 6: An auditable decision (2:32-3:00)

What we deliver is an auditable decision: go, no-go or inapplicable. A go
requires all six benefit comparisons and every safety gate. A no-go is useful
because it stops weak or merely cytoprotective ideas before scarce rare-disease
resources go into translational work. If the private mechanism does not match
this model, the answer is inapplicable and we do not force a fit. We claim no
treatment. We make it much harder to mistake cell survival for rescue.
